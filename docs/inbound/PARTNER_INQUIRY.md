# T-OCS V7 — Partner Inquiry Guide (Public L0)

**Purpose:** Make it straightforward for qualified engineering, test, manufacturing, OEM/Tier-1, research, and strategic partners to initiate a discussion about T-OCS V7.0 REVISION-2.

## Current stage

T-OCS V7 is a rear-seat occupant-control system concept in engineering definition and evidence-closure work. The near-term objective is to scope the next credible engineering step with a qualified partner—not to represent the concept as validated or production-ready.

## Partner capabilities sought

- Seat-system and automotive engineering-definition review
- CAE/simulation feasibility and technical gap closure
- Physical test-article and fixture design/fabrication
- Dynamic or sled-test planning, instrumentation, and measurement
- Manufacturing, automotive supplier, OEM/Tier-1 evaluation
- Applied research, co-development, licensing, or milestone-based strategic support

## How to start

**Primary route — email:** [naifautomotivesafetylab@gmail.com](mailto:naifautomotivesafetylab@gmail.com)

Please include:
1. Organisation and your role.
2. Which capability above is relevant.
3. The contribution your organisation could realistically offer.
4. A practical next step (e.g., a short scoping call, feasibility review, or request for a non-confidential brief).

**Optional public route:** [Open a non-confidential partner inquiry on GitHub](https://github.com/NAIF-Automotive-Safety-Lab/Car-seat-engineering/issues/new?template=partner-capability.yml).

The GitHub issue route is public. Use it only for non-confidential information; an issue must never contain private contact details, proprietary drawings, geometry, mechanism descriptions, unpublished test data, trade secrets, or other sensitive material. Email is preferred for first contact, but email is not itself a secure channel for sensitive files. Agree on an appropriate controlled-transfer method and, where appropriate, an NDA before any confidential disclosure.

## Evidence and disclosure boundary

The intended development path is engineering definition → physical test article and fixture → dynamic/sled testing → measurement and correlation → validation. Later stages are future or partner-dependent unless separately supported by evidence.

Public material is L0 only. This guide does not claim validated safety performance, certification, regulatory compliance, production readiness, OEM endorsement, customer traction, or a particular patent status. No confidential design detail is required to start a discussion.
