  1. Executive Summary
  The V6 disclosure treats the invention as a vehicle-seat architecture that controls occupant trajectory and the time-history of
  force/energy transfer. The system uses a mechanically distinct ride-down path for controlled seat translation and a separate
  rotation-control path for the seatback. A multi-state mechanism changes the seat between normal, armed/capture, ride-down,
  rebound-control and secure states. The intended benefit is not creation of a new gravitational field; the effective-gravity
  language is retained only as an inertial-frame interpretation.
  V6 inventive center: a coordinated combination of state transition, seat translation, independent seatback rotation control, redundant bilateral
  load paths, and explicit rebound control.

  The original project materials support this engineering direction. The V5 design report defines a dual-rail carriage, staged
  compliance, rotation control, pelvic anti-submarining geometry, and dedicated rebound control, while explicitly classifying the
  geometry as a research prototype. ■filecite■turn9file0■L681-L681■

  pulse and a reduction in seatback rotation from 47.5 degrees to 24 degrees in a strengthened model, with reduced ramping

  6. Detailed Mechanism Disclosure
  A. Dual-rail ride-down carriage: the seat carriage translates relative to the vehicle structure through a guided path. Each side
  carries a defined portion of the reaction load and can include an absorber or a redundant structural path.

  B. Independent seatback rotation control: the seatback frame is constrained by a separate bilateral path that can include
  links, dampers, torsional elements or hard stops. This path is intentionally not identical to the energy-stroke path.

  C. Multi-state lock: the lock mechanism is disclosed generically to permit cam/pawl, wedge, dog-clutch, pin, ratchet or
  equivalent constructions. The inventive point is its state coordination with ride-down and rebound, not the existence of an
  inertial pawl itself.

  D. Energy absorber: the replaceable cartridge is characterized by F(x), F(t), hysteresis and temperature response. The design
  package uses the nominal 180 mm concept stroke, but this remains a design assumption until derived from a vehicle-specific
  crash pulse and validated energy balance. ■filecite■turn5file0■L365-L370■

  E. Occupant trajectory: the system is disclosed as a coupled seat + restraint + occupant problem. The CAE observables
  include seat travel, seatback rotation, head excursion, pelvis travel/rotation, reaction forces and absorber work.
  F. Effective-gravity language: the equation g_eff(t)=g-a_seat(t) is retained as an inertial-frame interpretation only. It must not be described
  as a local gravitational field generator. ■filecite■turn9file0■L655-L655■

  10. Rear-Seat Embodiments - 2+1 and 3-Seat
  2+1: two outboard T-OCS modules can use dedicated carriage/absorber/load paths; the center seat can use a narrower or
  shared structural architecture while preserving independent occupant restraint and appropriate egress/packaging.

  3-seat: three independently managed modules can share selected cross-members while keeping independent
  absorption/locking functions. The claim set should not require exactly three seats; seat count is packaging, not the inventive
  center.

  Commercial rationale: the same state-transition logic can be packaged into sedan, SUV, crossover, MPV and
  autonomous-vehicle interiors. That gives the manufacturer a platform architecture rather than a single seat SKU.
  Ruthless caveat: the industrial-design renderings are design-intent visualizations. They do not prove H-point compliance, belt anchorage
  compliance, occupant clearance, manufacturability, or crash performance.

  12. Safety / Fail-Safe Disclosure
  The primary occupant restraint path remains available even if the optional ride-down trigger does not arm. The system should
  default to a mechanically safe state when trigger, sensor, actuator, or power availability is lost.

  Required design reviews before any human-subject or vehicle-crash use:
  • Single-point failure and common-cause failure analysis for the lock/trigger.

  • Mechanical stop analysis at both stroke ends.

  • Over-travel and jam analysis for both rails.

  • Asymmetric-load analysis for one-sided absorber or link degradation.

  • Temperature and contamination sensitivity of seals, springs, damping fluid and locks.

  16. Pre-Filing Gate - Do Not Skip
  • Confirm the true earliest conception date and preserve dated CAD files, notebooks, sketches and revisions.

  • Identify every public disclosure already made and obtain patent counsel advice on priority, foreign rights and any applicable
  grace-period rules.

  • Run a professional novelty and inventive-step search beyond the preliminary Google Patents screen.

  • Create a claim chart mapping each V6 limitation against each relevant prior-art document.

  • Convert visual boards into consistent patent drawings with stable reference numerals.

  • Replace every nominal CAD dimension with a vehicle-package value, measured prototype value, or explicit assumption.

  • Perform V0 CAD integrity and interference review.

  • Perform V1 multibody dynamic screening.

  • Characterize V2 absorber F-x/hysteresis/temperature/cycling.

  • Bench-test V3 lock/trigger including fault and unintended-release cases.

  • Run V4 sled tests with appropriate ATDs and high-speed video.

  • Correlate V5 FE/HBM models to physical data before making injury-reduction claims.

  • Use full-vehicle testing for V6 vehicle-level compliance and safety evidence.
